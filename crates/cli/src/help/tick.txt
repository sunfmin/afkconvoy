WHEN TO RUN IT

  For exactly one reconciliation pass that exits: to step a fleet by hand and read what
  each pass did, or where a long-lived process cannot be kept. `run` is this in a loop.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  What one tick of `run` does: the startup probe, the rebuild, then every transition the
  working set calls for — on the remote, on GitHub and in orca. It does not wait for the
  workers it starts, and keeps nothing on this host a later tick needs. Like `plan` it
  makes no probe while one that passed on this host in the last hour stands, and says
  so on stderr (`afkconvoy plan --help`) — one that checked the worker launch command
  this tick starts its workers with: another command is probed for.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"did", "errors", "held", "frontier_remaining", "locks"}

    did                 each step taken: {do, issue, outcome}. do is settle, tend_train,
                        judge, watch, advance, escalate, end_patrol, patrol,
                        end_proposal, propose, unlock, reclaim, dispatch, board,
                        stage_label or heartbeat; outcome is done, nudged, continued,
                        restarted, abandoned, nothing, lost or failed. A step on a
                        patrol has a null issue and names it: {do, issue, patrol,
                        outcome}. A step on the proposal of a precedent names the
                        issue that was ruled on. An `advance` that read a verdict and
                        left the issue at a stage names it: {do, issue, outcome,
                        stage} — `land` once the last stage passed to the landing train
    errors              each step that failed: {do, issue, error}, with `patrol` as
                        in `did`
    held                the claims the instance holds after the pass, a patrol's run
                        and a proposal still going among them
    frontier_remaining  the frontier issues it left undispatched
    locks               the fleet locks that stood as it ran: the open issues carrying
                        the lock label — none: []. `run` opens its line with them:
                        "FLEET LOCK #7 stands: only it is worked"

  Errors: a command the fleet needs is missing or logged out, or orca is not running
  or does not know this checkout (the error names each, with the line to run); the repo fails the startup probe; the config, GitHub or the
  remote cannot be read; the worker launch command resolves to nothing, or — in a bound
  repo — does not take the flags that start a worker in its role; `--instance` is
  not an instance id ([a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{0,39}).

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required: `run` mints an id, a tick has to be given one. Give every
    tick of one fleet the same id — a new id holds no claims, and sees the old id's as
    another instance's.
  - In a bound repo the tick that reads a verdict ends the claim its stage was
    performed under (`advance`) and, where the verdict leads to a stage a role
    performs, starts that stage's worker into the slot it freed, under a claim of
    its own (`dispatch`, on the same issue): a `pass` to the next stage, a `reject`
    to an earlier one, a ruling that runs a stage again. That worker goes ahead of
    everything else the tick takes a slot for. Only a fleet over its `concurrency`
    has no slot for it: the issue is then on the frontier of the tick after, ahead
    of the issues no stage has declared on yet.
  - The last stage is different: the tick after a `pass` that leads to `land` starts a
    worker in the workflow's landing role, which gates the PR and joins the landing
    train; a `pass` to `land` with no open PR to join is handed to a human. A consult
    and a stage that runs again once a fleet lock closed are the tick after's too.
  - `run` does not sleep across that hop: after a tick that settled an issue or
    advanced one without starting what follows, and left a slot free, it starts the
    next tick at once (`next now` in its line), at most 4 ticks back to back. Ticking
    by hand, the next `tick` is yours to run.
  - An `escalate` does not always hand the issue to a human. From a stage that names a
    consult (`escalate: { to, do }`), the tick that reads it ends the stage's claim,
    and the next one starts a worker in the consult's role — under a claim of its
    own, in one slot of `concurrency`, in the same worktree. Its ruling (`afkconvoy
    rule`) runs the stage that asked again, in the tick that reads it, and counts as one more of
    the issue's rework in total; its own `escalate` goes to a human. A consult that
    stays silent is nudged once and then escalated, like any worker.
  - A `reject` does not always start the earlier stage again. Where it would pass one
    of the workflow's breakers — `max_rework`, `max_consecutive_rework` — the tick
    escalates the issue straight to a human instead, whatever consult the stage names;
    the comment names the breaker and lists every round's stage and reason in order.
    `afkconvoy rehearse` trips them at the same verdict.
  - A verdict is not carried out when its stage left what it may not have: a stage
    marked `writes: false` whose branch holds a commit that was not there when its
    worker started has failed, and the issue escalates whatever it declared. The same
    holds for a consult, whatever its stage is marked: it writes no code. The guard
    (`afkconvoy guard --help`) refuses such a commit as it is made; this is the check
    of what a guard missed.
  - A patrol the repo names (`patrols`, `afkconvoy config --help`) is run by the tick
    that finds it due: its interval has passed since its last recorded run, and no run
    of it is still going. The tick claims the run on the remote — of several hosts one
    wins, and the rest say `lost` — starts a worker in the patrol's role, in a checkout
    of the base branch as it stands, linked to no issue, and records the run. A run
    takes one slot of `concurrency`. A patrol is not run while its open issues have
    reached its `max_open`, nor while a fleet lock stands; one that missed runs is run
    once. `afkconvoy plan` says how each stands.
  - A patrol's worker declares no verdict: it declares its run over (`afkconvoy over
    --help`), with the line its brief ends with, and the next tick ends the run
    (`end_patrol`) and frees its slot. One that never does is ended once its worker is
    gone, or has been stopped for the grace period. Either way a run that left a commit
    in its checkout, or opened a PR, has failed: the tick opens an issue that says so, with the team's escalate label,
    and touches no other issue. The patrol is not run again until a human closes it.
  - A human's ruling on an escalated issue is proposed as a precedent, where the team
    names who proposes (`precedents`, `afkconvoy check --help`): a tick starts one
    worker in that role, with that skill, in a checkout of the base branch linked to no
    issue (`propose`). The worker opens one PR on the team's repo that appends one
    entry to `precedents.md` — the ruling in a sentence, when it applies, and the link
    to the ruling. A human merging it is what accepts the precedent: the fleet merges
    nothing on the team's repo. A verdict a human declares for a `by: human` stage is
    no ruling, and neither is a consult's.
  - The issue does not wait for the proposal: the stage that asked is dispatched
    first, and a proposal takes a slot of `concurrency` only where one is left. A
    ruling is proposed at most once — a comment on the issue says it was, before the
    worker is started — and none is started while the open proposals on the team's
    repo have reached `max_open`, nor while a fleet lock stands. `afkconvoy plan`
    says how each stands.
  - A proposal's worker declares its run over as a patrol's does, and the next tick
    ends the proposal (`end_proposal`) — or a later one does, once its worker is gone
    or has been stopped for the grace period: a PR from its branch that changes anything but `precedents.md`, or does
    anything there but append that one entry, is closed with the reason in a comment;
    one that is a proposal is left for a human. The checkout is removed.
  - With a fleet lock standing a tick works lock issues only (`afkconvoy lock --help`).
    It dispatches nothing else, runs no patrol, proposes no precedent, takes no stale claim of another issue, hands no other
    issue to a human, and leaves alone every claim of its own on another issue whose PR
    is not on the landing train: no judge, no watch, no advance, no nudge. What those
    workers declared is acted on by the first tick after the last lock issue closed.
    Their claims take no slot meanwhile, so `held` may pass the config's
    `concurrency` by as many; the line of `run` shows them apart, as `left alone`.
  - What a closed lock explains is run again, not carried out. In a bound repo, a stage
    whose own worker declared `escalate` before the lock closed and was not acted on
    since — and one whose worker stayed silent past a nudge sent before it closed — is
    not handed to its consult or to a human: the fleet leaves a stage record
    (`verdict=lock-closed by=fleet`), the claim is released (`advance`, `done`), and the
    next tick starts a worker on that stage, whose brief says a lock stood. It is no
    rework. A `pass`, a `blocked` and an `already-satisfied` stand as they are. What landed is still settled, and the train still tended.
  - A step that failed is not a failed command: the exit is 0 and the failure is in
    `errors`. Read `errors`, not the exit code.
  - It does not take the claims stamped with this host: only `run` does that, at its
    start. A tick takes another instance's claims only once they are stale.
  - Do not tick as an instance whose `run` is alive: both would act on the same claims.
