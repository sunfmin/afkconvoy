WHEN TO RUN IT

  To move work onto another harness: a harness's login expired, its provider turned
  access off, or another agent should take the work over. One line does it all:

    afkconvoy switch --instance <id> --harness codex --all

  It works on a worker that is busy, idle at its prompt, blocked from receiving
  input, or already gone. It needs no nudge first, no grace period and no PR.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, on the host the workers run on.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  The harness is this host's choice for the repo, kept in the repo's git dir on this
  host and never pushed: whoever else works the repo runs the harness they have,
  under their own accounts. From then on every worker this host starts for the repo
  — new issues, later stages, consults, patrols, the train worker, inspections — is
  of that harness, whatever the config's `harness` key, a stage or a patrol names.
  `--harness config` forgets the choice: the config's say stands again.

  With no ISSUE, `--patrol` or `--all`, only the choice is made: running workers are
  left on the harness they run.

  Then it moves the workers named:

  ISSUE      the worker of the instance's claim on that issue
  --patrol   the worker of the instance's run of that patrol, in the run's checkout
  --all      every claim of the instance on this host — on an issue, on a patrol's
             run — one after another; a failure on one is reported and the rest go on

  For each, it ends the worker — its agent and its terminal — and starts one of the
  harness in the same worktree, on the same branch, at the same stage: a consult as
  a consult, a landing worker as a landing worker. The claim, the branch, the
  worktree and the PR are untouched. The new worker's brief says it continues
  earlier work, and names what is there: the commits on the branch ahead of the
  base branch, the open PR, and the last lines the old worker's terminal showed.

  The switch is recorded on the PR, in the fleet's comment that records its join:
  from which harness to which, by which instance, and when. It is no restart, no
  rework and no nudge: no breaker count moves, and a joining worker keeps its one
  restart.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  For ISSUE: {"issue", "from", "to", "worktree", "branch", "terminal", "pr", "stage",
  "chosen"} — from and to are the old and new harness; chosen is {"was", "now"}, this
  host's choice before and after (null: the config's).

  For --patrol: {"patrol", "from", "to", "worktree", "terminal", "chosen"}.

  For --all, and with nothing named: {"harness", "chosen", "switched": [each as
  above], "failed": [{"issue" or "patrol", "error"}]}.

  Errors, each with nothing chosen and no worker ended: the harness cannot be started
  here — missing, logged out, or its command not taking the flags its role needs —
  named with what is wrong; the instance holds no claim on the issue, or no run of
  the patrol; the claim is another instance's.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required, and must be the instance that holds the claims.
  - The choice holds on this host until a switch chooses again: a change of the
    config's `harness` key moves nothing here while it stands.
  - A claim whose stage has a verdict declared and not yet acted on is refused: the
    next tick acts on that verdict, and the worker it starts is of the chosen harness.
