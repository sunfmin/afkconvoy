WHEN TO RUN IT

  To close, by hand, a claim whose issue needed no change. A tick does this on its own
  for a worker that stopped with an `already-satisfied` verdict and no work on its
  branch. Do it yourself when a plan keeps showing such a claim under `mine` as `no_pr`.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, on the host the worker ran on.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It closes the issue on GitHub, writes the status board's last word, releases the claim
  on the remote, and removes the worktree.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "action": "closed", "released": true}
  {"issue", "released": true, "detail"} — no claim is held on the issue: nothing was done.

  Errors: the claim is another instance's ("is not this fleet's claim"); the worktree
  shows work, or there is no worktree on this host to show there is none ("is not closed
  as needing no change"). Nothing was changed.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required, and must be the instance that holds the claim.
  - It does not read the verdict. Any claim of the instance whose worktree has no commit
    past the base branch and nothing uncommitted is closed — one whose worker has only
    just started as well.
