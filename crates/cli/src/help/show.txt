WHEN TO RUN IT

  After `check` accepts a team, to review a workflow as a whole: where an issue enters,
  which role performs each stage, and where every `pass` and `reject` leads. Paste it
  into a GitHub comment inside a code block to have it reviewed there.

  With `--issue`, to ask where one issue is in its workflow: its position, what follows
  it, and the rework it has left before a breaker stops it.

      afkconvoy show --issue 7

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, or whoever designs the team: in a checkout of a bound repo, or anywhere
  with `--team` or `--dir`.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It writes nothing. It reads a team as `check` does, with the same `--team` and `--dir`:
  the team's files, and every role's repo from GitHub. See `afkconvoy check --help`.
  With a workflow's name it draws that one; with none, every workflow of the team.

  With `--issue` it also reads, in a checkout of the issue's repo, the issue's labels
  and its comments. The workflow is the one the issue's flow label names — for an
  escalated issue, whose flow label was swapped out, the one its escalation's comment
  says it left. The position is read off the issue's stage records — the comments
  `afkconvoy verdict`, `rule` and `send` write — and off nothing else: not its `stage:` label, which the fleet writes for
  humans to filter by and never reads.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  Not JSON: a drawing, as plain text. One workflow:

      feature — flow:feature — max_rework 3, max_consecutive_rework 2

      ┌───▶ plan      dev: plan
      │       ↓ pass → build
      │ ┌─▶ build     dev: implement · model opus · escalate → reviewer: rule
      │ │     ↓ pass → review
      │ └── review    reviewer: review · writes nothing · reject → build
      │       ↓ pass → sign-off
      └──── sign-off  human · reject → plan
              ↓ pass → land
            land      dev: land

  The first line is the workflow, its flow label and its two breakers. Then one row per
  stage, in the order its file lists them — an issue enters at the first — with who
  performs it, as `<role>: <skill>` or `human`, and what else its file sets. Under each
  stage is where its `pass` leads. A `reject` is said on its stage's row and drawn as
  the line that leaves that row to the left and comes back in, at the arrow, to the
  stage it leads to. The last rows are the ends: `land` with who lands, and `done`.

  With `--issue`, JSON:

  {"issue", "workflow", "position", "follows", "rework", "consecutive_rework", "records"}

    position            where the issue is: {"is", "stage", "by", "do", "reason"}, as
                        `afkconvoy rehearse --help` spells out
    follows             the position a `pass` declared now would lead to; null where
                        none can be declared: the issue is landing, ended, escalating
                        or with a consult
    rework              {"had", "left"}: its rework so far, and what `max_rework` still
                        allows
    consecutive_rework  for each stage that may reject, {"had", "left"}: its rejections
                        in a row, and what `max_consecutive_rework` still allows
    records             its stage records, oldest first: {"stage", "verdict", "reads",
                        "by", "claim", "role_commit", "team_commit", "url"}

  Of a record, `by` says who left it: `worker`, under the `claim` and the two commits
  it names, or `human`, for whom all three are null. `verdict` is what it states: one
  of the five verdicts, `ruling`, or `sent`. And `reads` says how it counted:

    verdict        it is the verdict of the stage its claim was dispatched to, or what
                   a human declared or ruled at the stage the issue was at
    sent           a human sent the issue to its stage
    other-stage    it names a stage the issue was not at: it counts for nothing
    claim-spent    a verdict was already declared under its claim: it is not one
    unknown-stage  it names a stage the workflow no longer has: the issue escalates

  Errors, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit: the team has a fault — {"error",
  "faults"}, as `check` answers it; the team has no workflow of that name, or none at
  all; the issue has no position — it carries several flow labels, one that names no
  workflow of the team, or none with no escalation that says which it left; and every
  error of `check`.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - It draws only a team that loads. Run `check` first: a fault is reported, not drawn.
  - `blocked`, `already-satisfied` and `escalate` are not drawn: they mean the same at
    every stage. `afkconvoy rehearse` shows where each leads.
  - `--issue` reads the team as it stands now, not as it stood when a record was
    written: a workflow changed since can leave records that name no stage of it.
