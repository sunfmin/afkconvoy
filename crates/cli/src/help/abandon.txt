WHEN TO RUN IT

  To give the landing train up by hand. A tick does this on its own when the train's
  worker stopped with the train not landed, was nudged, and stayed silent for a grace
  period more. Do it yourself when a tick is not running and the train stands still.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, on the host the train worker runs on.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  On the remote it deletes the landing train — only while its tip is still the one its
  worker went silent on. It clears the train's worktree and keeps it. For each PR of the
  instance that was on the train it records the abandonment in the fleet's comment on the
  PR and tells the PR's worker to join the next train; a PR taken off an abandoned train
  before is escalated instead. Nothing has landed, and what an earlier train put on the
  base branch is not touched.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"action": "abandoned", "train", "issues", "escalated", "worktree"}

    train      the tip of the train given up
    issues     the issues whose PRs are to join the next train
    escalated  the issues escalated instead
    worktree   the train's worktree, kept

  Errors, each with nothing abandoned: "nothing is on the landing train"; "has no
  worktree on this host"; "is not abandoned" — its worker is at work, gone, not told of
  what the train holds now, not nudged yet, or showed a sign of life within the grace
  period; "moved while it was being abandoned" — a PR joined meanwhile, and the train
  was kept.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required. Only that instance's PRs are told to join again.
  - It is refused wherever a tick would not abandon: it cannot be used to clear a train
    whose worker is still at it.
  - It takes no `--issue`: a repo has one landing train.
