WHEN TO RUN IT

  Once, as the last command of an inspection. Its brief ends with the exact line. An
  inspection is of one of two kinds, and finds in words of its own.

  Of a hot worker — one whose processes take more than their share of the host — to
  find out what takes so much and why. It found one of three things:

    wrong     the work is wrong: say what is wrong and where
    heavy     the work really is that heavy: say which command is
    not-its   what takes so much is not the worker's: a daemon outside its tree,
              another fleet's gate

  Of the host, once admission has held back new workers for one reason — the load,
  memory or disk — past its span (`afkconvoy run --help`, ADMISSION), to find out what
  holds what admission read and why. It found one of three things:

    reclaimable  the fleet holds what it no longer needs — a worktree whose issue is
                 closed, the build output of a worktree whose next stage rebuilds it
                 anyway: name each, with how much
    fleet        the fleet's own shape outgrows the host — its concurrency times one
                 worktree's fresh build past the free disk, say: say the arithmetic
    not-its      what holds it is outside this fleet — another fleet, a daemon, a
                 user's files: name it

WHO RUNS IT

  A worker started as an inspection, in its own worktree: the checkout it was started in
  (`afkconvoy-inspection`). Run anywhere else it is refused, and so is a word of the
  other kind.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It keeps what was found in that checkout's own git dir — a second run says it again,
  and the last one stands — and wakes the `run` of this host (`afkconvoy wake --help`).
  That `run` ends the inspection at its next look, within 30 seconds, and removes its
  checkout, its terminal with it.

  Of a hot worker, it types what was found at the worker's terminal — not `not-its` —
  telling it to fix the work, or to run the heavy command through `afkconvoy exclusive`
  from now on, and leaves a comment on the worker's issue saying what was found and why,
  which is no stage record. The issue stays at its stage, and the worker keeps working.

  Of the host, it keeps what was found in the cache every fleet of the host shares
  (host-inspection/; `afkconvoy help cache`), shown by the panel and `doctor` beside
  admission's reading. It deletes nothing on its word. For `fleet` it opens one issue
  in the repo saying so — with the escalate label and the label fleet-outgrows-host —
  and none while one carrying that label is open.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"inspected": true, "found", "kind", "woke"} — what was found is kept; `kind` is worker
  or host; `woke` is whether a `run` on this host was told.

  Errors: the text is empty; the checkout it runs in is no inspection's; the word is
  not one its kind finds.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - An inspection is given 15 minutes. One that has said nothing by then is ended:
    nothing is typed at the worker, and the comment, or the host's record, says it
    found nothing in time.
  - It types nothing itself, and opens no issue: the `run` does. An inspection that
    does as well does it twice.
  - An inspection is held as a consult: it commits, pushes and changes the host never
    (`afkconvoy guard --help`).
