WHEN TO RUN IT

  To send a worker its nudge by hand. A tick does this on its own, once, for a worker
  that stopped with no PR and no verdict and stayed quiet for the grace period. Do it
  yourself when a tick is not running and such a worker sits idle.

WHAT COUNTS AS SILENT

  A worker is silent when it stopped, declared nothing, showed no sign of life — its
  terminal's output, a commit, a file touched, being started or nudged — for the
  grace period (300s), and waits on no background work of its own. Silent, it is
  nudged once; silent a grace period past that nudge, it is escalated (a joining
  worker is restarted first, a train worker's train abandoned).

  A worker whose turn ended while a shell or a monitor it started in the background
  still runs — its gate, say — is waiting, not silent: its harness shows that work
  in the footer under its prompt (Claude Code's `1 shell, 1 monitor`), which the tick
  reads off its screen, and the tick's line says `waiting on its own background work
  #7 (since …)`. It is neither nudged nor escalated while it waits. Once that work
  ends, its silence is counted as ever: a nudge, then escalated past it. Waiting
  longer than the gate limit (`gate_seconds`) is a silence at once, nudged or not,
  and the escalation says it waited on its own background work past the limit.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, on the host the worker runs on.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It asks orca for the worker's state and reads the issue's comments for a verdict. It
  types one line at the worker's terminal and records the nudge in the worktree. Nothing
  on GitHub or the remote changes.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "action": "nudged", "terminal"}

  Errors, each with nothing sent: "has no stopped worker" — the instance holds no claim
  on the issue, or its worker is at work or gone; "is not nudged" — it was nudged
  already, showed a sign of life within the grace period, waits on background work
  of its own, or declared a verdict; the
  claim is another instance's; the worktree has no live terminal.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required, and must be the instance that holds the claim.
  - It is refused wherever a tick would not nudge: it cannot be used to hurry a worker.
  - One nudge per worker. A worker silent again after it is escalated by the next tick.
