WHEN TO RUN IT

  To put an issue of a bound repo at a stage of your choosing, whatever its records say:
  back to planning after a review that missed the point, past a stage you have done by
  hand, or out of an escalation to another stage than the one that asked.

      afkconvoy send --issue 7 --stage plan --text "The approach changed: see #12."

  The issue is then at that stage, to be performed, with both rework counts started
  over. To answer an escalated issue where it stopped, `afkconvoy rule` is the command.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, or whichever human steers the issue: in a checkout of the bound repo.
  No worker runs it: where an issue goes is its workflow's to say, or a human's.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It reads the team the config names, the claims on the remote, and the issue's labels
  and comments. The workflow is the one the issue's flow label names; for an escalated
  issue, the one its escalation's comment says it left.

  It writes one comment on the issue — a stage record that names the stage, says a
  human sent the issue there and carries the text — and wakes the fleet. An escalated
  issue also gets its flow label back and loses the team's `escalate_label`, after the
  record is written. Nothing else moves: the next tick reads the issue's position off
  its records, starts a worker on the stage if a role performs it, and waits for a
  human's verdict if the stage is `by: human`.

  The text is `--text`, or the contents of `--text-file`: one of the two, not empty.
  The worker started on the stage is shown it in its brief.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "workflow", "stage", "sent": true, "relabelled", "woke"}

    relabelled  whether a label was moved: true for an issue that was escalated
    woke        whether a `run` on this host was told, as `afkconvoy wake` answers

  Errors, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit, with nothing written: the workflow
  "has no stage" of that name — the error lists the ones it has; the issue "is claimed"
  — a worker is at a stage of it; it "cannot be sent to a stage" — no flow label and no
  escalation says which workflow it follows; no text, or an empty one; the repo names
  no team; and every error of `afkconvoy check`.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - It is refused while the issue is claimed: a claim is dispatched to one stage, and
    its worker would go on performing it. Wait for the stage to declare, or hand the
    claim to a human with `afkconvoy escalate` first.
  - It reopens nothing and closes nothing. An issue sent back from an ended workflow
    must be open to be worked.
  - The stage is named as its workflow's file names it. `afkconvoy show` draws them.
  - Run twice, it leaves two records; the issue is at the stage either way.
