WHEN TO RUN IT

  Once, as the last thing a stage does: it declares the verdict that ends the stage,
  with the text the stage produced. A worker's brief gives the line with `--issue` and
  `--stage` filled in; add the verdict and the text:

      afkconvoy verdict pass --issue 7 --stage build --text-file /tmp/build.md

  The five verdicts:

      pass               the stage did its work: the issue goes where the workflow says
      reject             the work of an earlier stage is turned down; the text says why.
                         Only a stage whose workflow gives it a `reject` may declare it
      blocked            the issue waits on other issues: name them with `--blocked-by`.
                         A stage that splits its issue declares it on the issues it
                         opened and took in (`afkconvoy intake --help`), and runs again
                         when they have all closed. So does a stage whose gate is red for
                         a defect outside its issue, on the defect's issue: one it opened
                         and took in, or the open one that already recorded it
      already-satisfied  the issue needs no change: it is already done
      escalate           the stage cannot decide; the text says what has to be decided

  Opening a PR declares nothing: a stage that wrote one still declares `pass` — after
  `afkconvoy gate --train` says green on what it pushed, as its brief tells it.

A consult also declares `close` or `park`, with its reason in the text. Close ends
  the issue; park leaves it open for new evidence, off the frontier until explicitly
  brought back (see `afkconvoy help humans`). Neither adds rework or resets its counts.
  `escalate` lets through to a human what the consult was asked to rule on. Its ruling
  uses `afkconvoy rule --help`. In an unbound repo the stock consult uses `--consult`
  and `--stage consult` with `close` or `park`, with no role or team commits.

  A human runs it too, for a stage a workflow gives them (`by: human`): the issue waits
  there, unclaimed, until they declare. `afkconvoy show --issue 7` names the stage:

      afkconvoy verdict pass --issue 7 --stage sign-off --text "Approved."

WHO RUNS IT

  A worker, in its own worktree: it starts in its role's directory, so the line its
  brief gives changes to the worktree first. The fleet put the commits of its role and
  of its team, and the harness it is a session of, in its environment, and the command
  reads them from there.

  Or a human, in a checkout of the bound repo, with neither commit named and none in
  the environment: that is what makes the record a human's.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It reads the claim on the issue off the remote and the issue's comments, and writes
  one comment on the issue: the stage record. The record opens with a marker that
  carries the stage, the verdict, the claim it was declared under, the commits of the
  role and of the team and the harness the worker ran on — and nothing of how it was
  started — and the text follows it. Nothing but this command writes that
  format.

  A human's record is written under no claim, and only on an issue nobody holds. It
  says a human left it and carries no commit. Unlike a worker's it is checked first,
  against the team the config names and the issue's records: the issue must be at the
  stage named, and the verdict one that may be declared there.

  The text is `--text`, or the contents of `--text-file`: one of the two, and not empty.
  Then it wakes the fleet, as `afkconvoy wake` does.

  It moves nothing. Where the issue goes is read by the fleet off its records and its
  workflow at the next tick: see `afkconvoy show --help`.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "stage", "verdict", "claim", "declared", "woke"}
  {"issue", "stage", "verdict", "by": "human", "declared": true, "woke"} — a human's

    declared: true   the record was written
    declared: false  this stage already has a record under this claim: nothing was
                     written, and `standing` is the verdict that record declared
    woke             whether a `run` on this host was told, as `afkconvoy wake` answers

  Errors, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit: a word that is none of the five
  verdicts; no text, or an empty one; `--blocked-by` with another verdict than
  `blocked`; a worker's on an issue that is not claimed; one of `--role-commit` and
  `--team-commit` (or AFKCONVOY_ROLE_COMMIT and AFKCONVOY_TEAM_COMMIT) without the
  other; a `--harness` (or AFKCONVOY_HARNESS) that names no harness, or one with no
  commit; "is not in a git checkout".

  A human's is refused, with nothing written, when the issue "is claimed" — its stage
  is its worker's, and a worker's record names both commits; when it is at another
  stage, or has left its stages; when the verdict "cannot be declared" there; with
  `--blocked-by`; and when the issue carries no one flow label.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - One stage declares once under one claim. A second run changes nothing, whatever it
    says: the first record stands.
  - A record is a verdict only for the stage the claim was dispatched to. One that
    names another stage is written and counts for nothing: the worker is then treated
    as having declared no verdict.
  - It does not check that the verdict is one the stage may declare. A `reject` from a
    stage whose workflow gives it none is no valid verdict, and the issue escalates.
  - A ruling is not a verdict, and is not declared here: see `afkconvoy rule --help`.
  - An `escalate` reaches a human straight only from a stage that says
    `escalate: { to: human }`. From any other, the next tick starts its consult — the
    role it names, or the stock consult where it names none — which rules — and the
    stage runs again under the ruling — or declares `escalate` itself. That one
    always goes to a human: the chain is one role long.
  - Whether a record is a consult's is read from AFKCONVOY_CONSULT, which the fleet
    put in the worker's environment (or `--consult`). Anything a consult declares but
    `escalate`, `close` or `park` is no valid verdict, and the issue escalates.
  - With no commit named a record is a human's. A worker whose environment lost its
    commits is refused, as its issue is claimed.
  - A human's `already-satisfied` ends the workflow and closes nothing, and a human's
    `blocked` parks nothing: no claim is there to do either.
