WHEN TO RUN IT

  When an issue of a bound repo was escalated and you have decided what it asked: the
  issue carries the team's escalate label in place of its flow label, and the comment
  above the label says why. Answer it with the ruling:

      afkconvoy rule 7 --text "Keep the v1 schema; migrate in a later issue."

  The issue's number is an argument, or `--issue 7`: one of the two. The comment of the
  escalation ends with this line, the number filled in, and `afkconvoy doctor` lists it
  for every issue that waits (`waiting_on_human`).

  The stage the issue stopped at runs again under the ruling, and both rework counts
  start over from it. `afkconvoy show --issue 7` says which stage that is before you
  rule. To put the issue at another stage instead, see `afkconvoy send --help`.

  Or as a consult: a stage that names one (`escalate: { to, do }` in its workflow)
  declared `escalate`, and the fleet started you in that role to rule on it before a
  human is asked. Your brief gives the line, with `--issue` and `--stage` filled in:

      afkconvoy rule --issue 7 --stage build --text-file /tmp/ruling.md

  The stage that asked runs again under your ruling. What is not yours to rule on you
  let through to a human instead, with `afkconvoy verdict escalate` — your brief gives
  that line too.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, or whichever human the issue was handed to: in a checkout of the bound
  repo, with no commit named and none in the environment.

  Or a consult, in the issue's worktree: the fleet put the commits of its role and of
  its team in its environment, and those are what make the ruling a consult's. No
  other worker runs it: a stage does not rule on itself.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It reads the team the config names, the claims on the remote, and the issue's labels
  and comments. The workflow is the one the issue's flow label names; with the label
  swapped out, the one its escalation's comment says it left.

  It writes one comment on the issue — a stage record that carries the ruling, says a
  human left it and names the stage — then puts the flow label back on and takes the
  team's `escalate_label` off, and wakes the fleet. The record is written first: once
  the flow label is back the issue is the fleet's again, and its next tick starts a
  worker on the stage, with the ruling in its brief.

  A consult's ruling is one comment too, and nothing else: a stage record that says the
  stage's consult left it, under the claim the consult holds on the issue, with the two
  commits. It moves no label — the issue never left the fleet — and the next tick ends
  the consult's claim; the one after starts the stage that asked, with the ruling in
  its brief. It is checked no more than a worker's verdict is.

  The ruling is `--text`, or the contents of `--text-file`: one of the two, not empty.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "workflow", "stage", "ruled": true, "relabelled", "woke"}
  {"issue", "stage", "verdict": "ruling", "claim", "declared", "woke"} — a consult's,
  as `afkconvoy verdict` answers a worker

    stage       the stage that runs again under the ruling
    relabelled  whether a label was moved; false when the issue already carried its
                flow label and no escalate label
    woke        whether a `run` on this host was told, as `afkconvoy wake` answers

  Errors, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit, with nothing written: the issue "is
  claimed" — its escalation is still under way, or a worker is at it; it "is not
  escalated" — its records put it at a stage and it carries its flow label; it "cannot
  be ruled on" — it has left the stages of its workflow, or no flow label and no
  escalation says which workflow it follows; no text, or an empty one; the repo names
  no team; and every error of `afkconvoy check`. A consult's: `--stage` is missing,
  or given with no commits; the issue "is not claimed".

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - A ruling runs the same stage again. It does not decide the stage's verdict: the
    worker started under it does.
  - An issue escalated because its worker went silent, or its gate stayed red, is at
    the stage that worker was performing: a ruling starts that stage anew.
  - An escalation made before its comment named the workflow cannot say which flow
    label to put back. Put the flow label on by hand, then rule.
  - Moving the labels by hand hands the issue back with no ruling on record: an issue
    its records show as escalating is escalated again at the next tick.
  - Run twice, it leaves two rulings. Both count: the stage runs again once. A
    consult's second changes nothing: one claim declares once.
  - A human's ruling starts both rework counts over. A consult's does not: it is one
    more of the issue's rework in total, and one that would pass `max_rework` escalates
    the issue instead of running the stage again.
  - A consult writes no code. The guard refuses its commits and pushes, and a commit it
    left on the branch all the same is a failure: the issue escalates, whatever it ruled.
