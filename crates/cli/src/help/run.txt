WHEN TO RUN IT

  To work the backlog: after `config` answers and `plan` shows the frontier you expect.
  Run it again to start a fleet that was stopped — it picks up where that one left off.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, in the background: it never returns.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  Everything the fleet does. It makes the startup probe — at every start, whatever
  probe passed before — takes every claim stamped with this host — on an issue, on a
  patrol's run, on the proposal of a precedent — removes the heartbeats that are dead
  and hold nothing (those of the instances it took from, this host's past the lease,
  anyone's older than a day) — then ticks: each tick rebuilds the
  working set from GitHub and the remote
  and makes every transition it calls for — claims and heartbeats on the remote, workers
  in orca worktrees, comments, labels and blocked-by edges on issues, status boards, the
  landing train's worker. Between ticks it sleeps; a worker's `wake` ends the sleep,
  and a tick that left the next one a worker to start is followed with no sleep.

  Once every fleet on this host has been idle ten minutes it puts the host to sleep
  (`pmset sleepnow`) — a host sleep — unless it was told `--no-host-sleep` (or
  AFKCONVOY_NO_HOST_SLEEP=true), which keeps the host awake for every fleet on it.
  Idle is holding no claim — on an issue, a patrol's run, a proposal — nothing on the
  landing train, and an empty frontier; an escalated issue and a patrol not yet due
  leave a fleet idle. Every `run` listens on a socket in the cache directory
  (instances/, named for its fleet instance) and removes it as it exits; one nobody
  listens on is removed by whoever finds it. Asked there, a `run` says its instance,
  repo and version, whether it was told `--no-host-sleep`, since when it has been idle
  or not, and when it last worked; asked to tick first, it ticks, then says so.

  ADMISSION. A tick starts no new worker while its host is busy: its 1-minute load
  average past 200% of its cores, less than 10% of its memory free (what the kernel
  counts as available: `sysctl kern.memorystatus_level`, or MemAvailable in
  /proc/meminfo), or less than 1% of the disk under the checkout free (`df`). The
  issue stays on the frontier for the next tick, and so do a stale claim, a due patrol
  and a due proposal. Workers already running are left alone, and so are the landing
  train and the worker a declared verdict starts. The host says its own shares, each a
  whole percent, in the environment of `run`, `tick`, `plan` and `doctor`:
  AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_LOAD (of its cores), AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_MEMORY and AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_DISK
  (free) — AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_LOAD=400 lets the load reach four times the cores, and
  AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_DISK=0 never holds for disk. A value that is no whole percent is an
  error. What cannot be read holds nothing back. `plan` and `doctor` say the
  thresholds in force and what the host reads.

  When admission has held back for one reason — load, memory or disk — at every tick
  for ten minutes in a row (AFKCONVOY_ADMIT_INSPECT_AFTER, in whole seconds, says the
  host's own span), `run` starts an inspection of the host: the stock consult, in a
  checkout of its own, given what was taken down as it started — admission's reading
  and shares; for disk, what each of this host's worktrees of every fleet holds, with
  its issue, its stage and whether a worker holds it, the landing train's among them,
  the fleet's caches and the largest directories of the whole volume under the home
  folder outside them (`du -x`, at most five minutes); for load or
  memory, the process trees of each worker and of the train, and the hottest processes
  outside them. Holding back for reasons that change starts none. It runs once an
  episode, which ends once admission has admitted for five minutes, and not where
  another fleet of the host inspected it in the same episode. It says what it found
  with `afkconvoy inspected`: reclaimable, fleet or not-its. For reclaimable it frees
  what can be rebuilt under the home folder — what git ignores in a checkout no process
  uses, a closed issue's worktree, a tool's cache — and names what cannot, never
  deleting it (`afkconvoy inspected --help`); for fleet it opens one issue in the
  repo, with the escalate label and the label fleet-outgrows-host, and no other while
  that one is open. What it found is a line of the log, and shown by the panel and
  `doctor` beside admission's reading.

  A `run` idle ten minutes checks then, tick due or not, and again ten minutes after
  a check that kept the host awake. It puts the host to sleep only when all hold:
    - it worked — held a claim, or had something on the landing train — since the
      host last woke (`sysctl kern.waketime`): a host woken, by hand or otherwise,
      stays awake until a fleet on it works again;
    - every `run` on the host answers within two seconds, none was told
      `--no-host-sleep`, and each has been idle ten minutes;
    - nobody touched the keyboard or mouse for ten minutes (HIDIdleTime, from `ioreg`);
    - every `run`, itself included, asked to tick, does so and still answers idle
      within two minutes.
  One that does not answer in time is not idle: the host stays awake this time, and
  is checked again later. A `run` of a version without the socket is not seen, and
  keeps nothing awake.

  It samples what each worker's processes take every ten seconds. As one turns hot
  (`afkconvoy help concepts`, Hot worker) it takes down what shows it — the process
  tree, a five-second `sample` of the hottest process, the resident memory of late,
  what its processes wrote, the largest directories of its worktree — and starts an
  inspection of it once per episode: a worker in the role of the stage's consult, in
  a checkout of its own, that says what it found with `afkconvoy inspected`. One
  inspection runs on this host at a time, across its fleets; it takes no slot of the
  `concurrency`, and is ended after 15 minutes. A host inspection is held to the same:
  of either kind, one runs on the host at a time, and a hot worker waiting for one goes
  first. While one runs, or a hot worker or the host waits for one, `run` sleeps 30
  seconds at most.

  It also keeps a log on this host, in a file under the cache directory (runs/;
  `afkconvoy help cache` says where it is and what moves it): one line when it
  starts, one as each tick starts — with why, and the command whose wake started it
  — and one as it ends — what `tick` answers for it — and one of each event that
  reached it: a wake heard during a tick, a check for host sleep, the host waking,
  being told to stop. Every command that changes fleet state writes a line of its
  own to the same file as it acts (its `--help` says so under WHAT IT LOGS); `run`
  does not print those. It keeps every line of the last 24 hours, and never fewer
  than 200 lines nor more than 5000. A line is written whole as it happens, so
  killing `run` loses none, and none a command appends while `run` cuts the file
  back is lost. Nothing reads the
  log to decide anything; `afkconvoy doctor` reads it to say what the last ticks did,
  which steps failed, and the wakes, starts, stops and host sleeps of late.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  On stdout, every line it writes to its log, as it is written and in the same order, until
  it is stopped; then, told to stop with SIGTERM or Ctrl-C, one `stop:` line and
  {"stopped": true, "ticks", "instance"}, with exit 0.

    start: run as tester-h1-1a2b3c4d on h1, its workers started with `claude`
    start: puts this host to sleep once every fleet on it has been idle ten minutes (`--no-host-sleep` keeps it awake)
    start: took #4, #7 stamped with this host        only when a start took or lost claims
    start: took #4, the run of the patrol sweep stamped with this host
    start: took #4 stamped with this host · swept 1 dead heartbeat(s) that held nothing
    stop: holding no claim, it took its heartbeat with it
    stop: it left its heartbeat with the claims it holds: the next start on this host takes them
    tick 1: started (start)
    tick 1: dispatched #12, #15 · holding 2/3 · frontier 4 · next in 30s
    tick 2: started (timer)
    woken by park #46 during tick 2: tick 3 follows
    tick 2: nothing to do · holding 2/3 · frontier 4 · next in 60s
    tick 3: advanced #12 → review · dispatched #12 · holding 2/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s
    tick 4: advanced #12 → land · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next now
    tick 5: dispatched #12 · holding 2/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s
    tick 6: escalated #15 (workup: consult let it through) · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next now
    tick 7: dispatched #71 · skipped #68 (blocked by #71) · holding 2/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s

  The second `start:` line is there unless it was told `--no-host-sleep`. Every tick
  has a line as it starts, before it does anything, naming why: `timer` (its sleep
  ran out), `woken` (a wake ended the sleep, or was heard during the tick before:
  `woken by` the command that sent it, and what that acted on, where it named them),
  `start` (the run's first) or `follow-up` (the tick before left a worker to start,
  or could not reach GitHub, so it runs with no sleep). A wake heard during a tick
  is a line as it is heard — the first of each tick — and the next tick starts as
  that one ends: ticks never overlap. A check for host sleep is a line, and so is
  the host waking from a sleep, as `run` notices it — a rest that took a minute past
  its length — and being told to stop:

    tick 4: started (woken by verdict #121)
    tick 4: started (woken)                          a wake that named nothing
    tick 5: started (follow-up)
    host sleep: every fleet on this host has been idle long enough, and ticked once more: put the host to sleep
    host sleep: kept awake: the keyboard or mouse was used 40s ago
    host sleep: kept awake: the host could not be put to sleep: <why>
    host woke: after 3600s asleep

  A tick's line names what it did — lost, escalated, released, left, settled, ended,
  advanced, unlocked, reclaimed,
  dispatched, skipped (a frontier issue found blocked just before its claim, with what
  blocks it), nudged, waiting on its own background work (with since when: `#7 (since
  2026-10-20 23:03 UTC, 12m ago)`), continued, restarted, tended the train, abandoned
  the train — then, when admission held back a worker it would have started, which
  threshold did (`admission holds back new workers: the load 31.20 is past 28.00,
  200% of its 14 cores`), then
  the claims held out of the concurrency, and the frontier left. The claims a fleet
  lock has it leave alone take no slot and are counted apart (`holding 1/3 · 2 left
  alone`), and so are those whose worker declared a verdict not acted on yet
  (`holding 3/3 · 1 declared`), as are those a start took past the bound (`holding 3/3 · 1 past the bound`). A step that failed is
  in the line as `FAILED <step> #<issue>: <why>`, and a tick that could not start as
  `tick N: FAILED: <why>`; the loop goes on either way. One cause that stopped several
  steps is said once: `FAILED 7 steps: <why>`.

  `advanced #12 → review`: the tick read a verdict that left #12 at `review`. Where a
  role performs that stage the same tick starts its worker, and says so: `dispatched
  #12`. An issue advanced to no stage — its workflow ended, or it was parked — is named
  alone.

  `escalated #15 (workup: consult let it through)`: the tick handed #15 to a human,
  whichever step did, with why in a few words — the stage first, where it was at one.
  It is named as nothing else. The comment on the issue says why in full and ends with
  the line that answers it; `afkconvoy doctor` lists it under `waiting_on_human`.

  A change made on GitHub, or by another host's fleet, shows on this host at the
  next tick, and in no other way: that tick reads GitHub and the remote, as every
  tick does. Nothing reaches a `run` sooner — no webhook, no port — and
  `afkconvoy wake` makes that next tick start now. Where the tick acts on such a
  change, its line says what changed since the tick before, and who changed it
  where that is known:

    tick 8: lost #46 to instance beta-1a2b3c4d on host h2 (takeover) · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s
    tick 9: dispatched #45 (labelled ready-for-agent since tick 8) · holding 2/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s
    tick 9: released #231 (the issue was deleted) · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s
    tick 9: left #97 (closed by @alice) · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next now
    tick 9: skipped #320 (blocked by #379, added since tick 8) · holding 1/3 · frontier 0 · next in 90s

  `lost #46`: a claim this instance held is another's now — taken by a takeover,
  or `(stale: past the lease)`, as a stale claim, this instance's heartbeat being
  past the lease. `released #231`: its issue was deleted, or `(the issue is gone
  from this repo)`, transferred. `left #97`: a claim with no PR whose issue was
  closed, by whoever GitHub says closed it (`closed on GitHub` where it says no
  one), is settled — nothing of the fleet's landed. `labelled …` and `added since
  …` say what the tick before saw otherwise. A change the tick does nothing about is
  in no line; nor is one made before the `run`'s first tick, which has no tick
  before it, nor anything of the kind in what `afkconvoy tick` answers.

  `next now` in place of `next in <n>s`: the tick settled an issue, or advanced one
  without starting what follows, and left a slot free, so what follows — a consult, the
  join of a PR, an issue the landing unblocked — is the next tick's to start, and that
  tick starts with no sleep.
  So does the tick after one that failed on a read GitHub could not be reached for
  (`GitHub could not be reached: …`): nothing says the next read fails too.
  At most 4 ticks run back to back; then it sleeps, whatever the last one left.

  With `--panel <port>` its first line is the panel's address, and it serves the panel
  there until it is killed: the fleet as a web page — every workflow as a line of its
  stages, the issues at work, queued and held back at each, the landing train — brought
  up to date in the browser after every tick, by itself. `afkconvoy panel --help` says
  what the page shows.

    panel: http://127.0.0.1:7070/

  Started without it, it serves the same page once `afkconvoy panel --open` asks it
  to, from any worktree of the repo on this host — while it sleeps or in the middle
  of a tick, which goes on — and then prints that line, wherever its ticks have got
  to. `panel --open` answers the address again later, of a panel opened either way.

  Errors, before the first tick, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit: the host is
  not macOS and it was not told `--no-host-sleep`; the panel's port is taken; a command the
  fleet needs is missing or logged out, or orca is not running or does not know this
  checkout (the error names each, with the line to run);
  the repo fails the startup probe; the config cannot be read; the host kit cannot be read; the worker launch command of a harness the
  fleet could be asked for resolves to nothing, or — in a bound repo — does not take
  the flags that start a worker in its role.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - Stop it with SIGTERM or Ctrl-C: it finishes the step it is in, makes none of the
    steps left in its tick, and exits — within a second when it sleeps. No claim is
    released on the way out and no worker is stopped: the workers work on, and the
    next `run` on this host takes the claims back. Holding no claim, it removes its
    heartbeat from the remote; holding one, it leaves it, and that next `run` removes
    it. A second signal ends it at once, as SIGKILL does, with no goodbye: what that
    leaves, a later start on this host removes once it is past the lease.
    A patrol's run and a proposal it takes back are ended by its first tick once their
    worker is gone or has stopped, as any run of its own.
  - Without `--instance` it mints a new instance id, named for this host, and its lines
    never print it. `afkconvoy doctor` finds it, and says it as `fleet.instance`, for
    `plan --instance` and the commands that make one transition by hand.
  - Its lines are gone with the terminal they were printed in. The same lines are in
    its log, a day of them at the least: `afkconvoy doctor` reads it back.
  - A second `run` on this host for this repo takes the first one's claims at its start.
  - The panel is served to this host only, and shows a tick, not this second: between
    two ticks it changes nothing but its countdown. It costs GitHub no read of its own.
  - Every worker launch command comes from the host kit: run `afkconvoy kit` to
    inspect or edit it. It is $XDG_CONFIG_HOME/afkconvoy/kit.json, or
    $HOME/.config/afkconvoy/kit.json, kept on this host and never pushed.
    `--worker-command` is gone. With no kit, `run` offers the same terminal setup
    as `kit`, and writes only on confirmation. Without a terminal it refuses,
    saying to run `afkconvoy kit`. A harness absent from the kit has no implicit
    stock command. Harness selection still follows a switch, a stage or patrol,
    then the repo's config.
  - Every worker is started under `nice` — as fast as before on a host with nothing else
    to do, and giving way to you and to the gate when it is busy — with its own
    `TMPDIR`: `.afkconvoy-tmp` in its worktree, which git ignores and which goes with it.
    Nothing else of an unbound repo's worker launch command is touched.
  - In a bound repo, before any worker is started, the command of every harness the
    fleet could be asked for is checked: the repo's default, each harness a stage of
    the team's workflows names, and each a patrol the repo runs names. One that
    cannot be started refuses the start, named with its harness and the stage or
    patrol that calls for it, and no worker at all is started: no harness is ever
    started in another's place.
  - A Claude Code worker of a bound repo starts in its role's directory, with the rest
    added to the worker launch command:
        cd <the role's copy> && export <the stage's facts> && <worker launch command>
          --add-dir <the issue's worktree> --add-dir <the team's copy>
          [--add-dir <the fleet's own, with yours>]
          --setting-sources project --settings <the fleet's> [--model <the stage's>]
    The role's rules, skills and hooks load because it starts there. The repo's own
    rules load from the added worktree, and its hooks do not; the team rules load from
    the team's copy. Your own skills and rules load too, unless the team file says
    `host_user: false` (`afkconvoy help team`): the third directory added is the
    fleet's own (harness/claude-code-host-user under the cache directory), and holds
    links to your ~/.claude/skills and ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md and nothing else. A skill of
    the role's wins over one of yours of the same name. Nothing of your settings, hooks,
    MCP servers or plugins is loaded either way. The settings added hold the guard and
    orca's status report, as hooks, and the answer to the bypass warning. A command you give must take `--add-dir`,
    `--setting-sources`, `--settings` and `--model`.
  - The stage's facts are in the environment of every worker of a bound repo, and a
    role's limits may read them: AFKCONVOY_ISSUE, AFKCONVOY_FLOW, AFKCONVOY_STAGE,
    AFKCONVOY_ROLE, AFKCONVOY_SKILL, AFKCONVOY_WRITES, AFKCONVOY_CONSULT,
    AFKCONVOY_BASE_BRANCH, AFKCONVOY_ESCALATE_LABEL, AFKCONVOY_LOCK_LABEL,
    AFKCONVOY_ROLE_COMMIT, AFKCONVOY_TEAM_COMMIT and AFKCONVOY_HARNESS. A patrol's
    worker has AFKCONVOY_PATROL in place of the first three.
  - A pi worker — of a stage or a patrol that says `harness: pi` — starts in its
    role's directory like any other, with what pi does not load from there added:
        cd <the role's copy> && export PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR=<the fleet's own>
          <the stage's facts> && <worker launch command> --approve --no-skills
          --skill <the role's copy>/.claude/skills [--skill <the fleet's own>/skills]
          --extension <the fleet's own>/afkconvoy.js [--model <the stage's>]
    `--approve` answers on the line what pi would ask at its terminal, and has it
    load the role's own limits (`.pi/extensions/`). The role's skills are named, then
    yours, and pi looks for no other; of two skills of one name pi keeps the first,
    the role's. pi's user directory is the fleet's own, not yours (~/.pi/agent): it
    holds links to your logins and to the models you defined (auth.json, models.json),
    a link to the extension orca installed to hear of a pi session's state, your skills
    and rules (skills, AGENTS.override.md, AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md), and nothing else of
    yours — no setting or other extension. Under a team file that says
    `host_user: false` it is another (harness/pi under the cache directory, not
    harness/pi-host-user), with none of your skills and rules, and yours are not
    named. The extension named
    is afkconvoy's own: it asks `afkconvoy guard` before every tool call, refusing
    one the guard refuses or cannot be asked of, and loads the rules of the issue's
    worktree (its AGENTS.md, or CLAUDE.md) and the team rules (the team's CLAUDE.md). A
    command you give for pi must take `--approve`, `--no-skills`, `--skill`,
    `--extension` and `--model`.
  - A Codex worker — of a stage or a patrol that says `harness: codex` — is the
    session you would have at a terminal, not `codex exec`: the fleet sends to a
    worker after it starts. It starts in its role's directory like any other:
        cd <the role's copy> && export CODEX_HOME=<the fleet's own>
          <the stage's facts> && <worker launch command> --add-dir <the worktree>
          --dangerously-bypass-hook-trust
          --config 'projects={"<the role's copy>"={trust_level="trusted"}}'
          --config 'project_doc_fallback_filenames=["CLAUDE.md"]'
          --config 'project_root_markers=[]'
          [--config 'developer_instructions="<the repo's own rules, the team rules>"']
          --config 'hooks={PreToolUse=[<what asks afkconvoy guard, and orca's status
            report>], <each other event a session's state changes at>=[<the report>]}'
          --config features.memories=false
          --config skills.bundled.enabled=false
          [--config 'skills.config=[<each skill of yours the role's stands for, off>]']
          [--model <the stage's>]
    `projects` and `--dangerously-bypass-hook-trust` answer on the line what Codex
    would ask at its terminal — whether it trusts the folder, and a review of its
    hooks — and have it load the role's own limits (`.codex/hooks.json`). The next
    two have it load its role's rules — the CLAUDE.md of the directory it starts in —
    and those of no directory above. Codex loads rules from no directory added to
    it, so `developer_instructions` says the other two to it, each as what it is and
    with its file: the repo's own rules — the worktree's AGENTS.md, or its CLAUDE.md
    where it holds none — and the team rules (the team's CLAUDE.md), as they stand
    when the worker starts. A repo and a team with none are told none. The hook
    named asks `afkconvoy guard` before every tool call: one the guard refuses, or
    cannot be asked of, is not made. It is on the line and in no file, so no worker
    starts without it. The report is the hook orca installed to hear of a Codex
    session's state (~/.orca/agent-hooks/codex-hook.sh), which orca names in your
    own hooks and a worker loads none of: it is given ten seconds, and refuses no
    call, whatever it does; where orca installed none, nothing is told, and a
    worker's state is read off its terminal. Codex's user directory is the fleet's own
    (harness/codex-host-user under the cache directory), not yours (~/.codex): it holds
    links to your login (auth.json), your skills (skills) and your rules
    (AGENTS.override.md, AGENTS.md), and nothing else of yours — no hook or
    configuration, so no provider you defined in config.toml. Under a team file that
    says `host_user: false` it is another (harness/codex), with your login alone. A login Codex
    keeps in a keyring is in no file, and is no worker's: the start refuses, and
    `doctor` says so. Its memories are off. Codex finds skills in `.agents/skills/`
    and `.codex/skills/` of the directory it starts in, and not in `.claude/skills/`,
    where a role's are: the role's copy a Codex worker starts in is Codex's own
    (<commit>-role+codex-<path>), and holds a link, `.agents/skills` to
    `../.claude/skills`, made once with the copy. The role's repo holds nothing for
    it; a role that holds either directory itself is left as it is, and a worker is
    shown the skills there. The last key leaves out the skills Codex carries. Codex
    shows both of two skills of one name, so a skill of yours named as one of the
    role's is turned off on the line, by the path of its file
    (`--config 'skills.config=[{path="<its SKILL.md>",enabled=false}]'`): the role's
    is the one a worker is shown. One thing of yours a Codex worker loads even under
    `host_user: false`: the skills of your home (~/.agents/skills). Codex finds them
    wherever its user directory is and has no way to be told to leave them out, and
    the fleet does not work around it — so on two hosts such a worker may be shown
    different skills besides its role's. Keep none there on a host whose Codex workers
    must be shown their role's alone. The
    stock command runs Codex in no
    sandbox: a worker is limited by the guard, and a host that wants a sandbox says
    a command of its own. A command you give for Codex must take `--add-dir`,
    `--dangerously-bypass-hook-trust`, `--config` and `--model`.
  - A stage's model is its harness's own name for it: pi's is `<provider>/<id>`. A
    stage on pi that names none gets whichever model pi picks for itself. A provider
    whose key is in your login shell's environment is reached too, and a rules file
    in a directory above the fleet's copies (~/AGENTS.md) is one pi loads.

  A DeepSeek Harness worker (`harness: dsh`) runs interactive `dsh-tui`, with
  `dsh` on PATH too. The stock command is `dsh-tui`; a host may name
  the `dsh` command in `afkconvoy kit`, which must accept leading `--patch`.
  It starts in its role's copy, with the stage facts and AFKCONVOY_HARNESS=dsh,
  DSH_HOME set to harness/dsh-host-user under the fleet's cache (harness/dsh
  with host_user: false), and the issue's worktree as the TUI's path argument.
  The fleet's DSH_HOME loads the stock base and TUI bundles, the role's own
  .dsh/cordis.patch.yml where present, and the fleet's --patch overlay last.
  Only credentials (.credentials.yaml), skills and rules (skills, AGENTS.md)
  are linked from your DSH_HOME (default ~/.dsh), beside the skills and AGENTS.md
  of DSH_AGENTS_HOME (default ~/.agents). The role's native skill provider ranks
  its canonical skills ahead of these user roots. Dependency resolution uses
  your installed dsh-tui dependencies; its manifest and patches,
  and your home patches, MCP servers and plugins, are never loaded or edited.
  A team or role may instead choose `dsh_profile: host-user` (independent of
  host_user): DSH_HOME stays the host user's (default ~/.dsh), DSH_AGENTS_HOME
  stays theirs (default ~/.agents), and dsh-tui loads their manifest, dependencies,
  plugins, MCP servers, theme, settings and both profile and home patches.
  This can differ across hosts and the profile may itself load user skills and
  rules even with host_user: false. The fleet writes no file there: its plugin
  stays in its cache and its --patch overlay is still last, after the role's
  limits, with required guard dependencies. User settings cannot disable it.
  The fleet's plugin supplies role, repo and team rules, role skills before
  yours, and the stage's model as written. It asks `afkconvoy guard` before
  every tool call and reports running/idle, including interruption, to orca.
  The TUI and agent loop require the guard plugin's loader service, so a
  missing or broken plugin starts neither. Ctrl+C interrupts the running turn
  and leaves the interactive session open.

WORKER PROXY BYPASS (HOST ONLY)

  --worker-no-proxy [HARNESS=]HOSTS says a comma-separated list of hosts
  workers reach directly. The bare form is for the
  default harness; repeat it for other harnesses, never twice for one. It is
  a setting of this start on this host, never a key of the repo's config.
  Example: --worker-no-proxy dsh=api.deepseek.com,internal.example

  The worker's login shell extends its exported no_proxy and NO_PROXY with
  this list and exports the joined list under both names before it starts
  the worker. Every other proxy variable stays as it was. Without a list,
  both spellings stay untouched, except DSH's stock api.deepseek.com addition.
  Say --worker-no-proxy dsh= to turn that stock addition off. These settings
  apply to every worker, including patrols, consults and the train worker.
  doctor and plan accept the same flag and show worker_no_proxy per harness:
  {harness, addition, no_proxy, NO_PROXY}. addition is null when no list is
  added; the other fields show the login shell's resulting comma-separated
  values. They describe a start now, with the flags given to that command.
