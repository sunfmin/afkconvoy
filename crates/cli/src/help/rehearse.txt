WHEN TO RUN IT

  While designing a workflow, to test it without starting a worker or spending anything:
  name the workflow and what is declared on an issue, in order, and it says where the
  issue would be after each and where it ends. Use it to see that a rejection leads
  back where you meant, and how many it takes to trip a breaker.

      afkconvoy rehearse feature pass pass reject pass pass

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, or whoever designs the team: in a checkout of a bound repo, or anywhere
  with `--team` or `--dir`.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  It writes nothing and starts nothing. It reads a team as `check` does, with the same
  `--team` and `--dir`: see `afkconvoy check --help`. No issue is read: the issue is one
  that has just entered the workflow, at its first stage.

  After the workflow's name comes what is declared, each by whoever the issue is with
  at that point. The five verdicts:

      pass               follows the stage's `on.pass`: to a stage, `land` or `done`
      reject             follows the stage's `on.reject`, back to an earlier stage
      blocked            parks the issue; the same stage runs again afterwards
      already-satisfied  ends the workflow
      escalate           goes to the stage's consult if its `escalate:` names one, else
                         to a human

  and `ruling`: what a consult, or a human on an escalated issue, decides. The stage
  that asked runs again under it.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"workflow", "max_rework", "max_consecutive_rework", "enters", "steps", "ends"}

    enters, ends   the position before anything is declared, and after everything
    steps          one per thing declared, in order: {"declared", "stage", "valid",
                   "breaker", "position", "rework", "consecutive_rework"}

  Of a step, `stage` is the stage it was declared in, null once the issue is landing
  or ended, and `position` where the issue is after it. A position is {"is", "stage",
  "by", "do", "harness", "reason"}:

    is: perform     a worker is to perform `stage`, as the skill `do` of the role `by`
    is: waiting     `stage` is a human's (`by: human`): it waits for their verdict
    is: consult     a worker is to rule on the `escalate` of `stage`, as `do` of `by`
    is: parked      parked at `stage`, which runs again when its blockers close
    is: landing     the workflow's `land` joins the landing train, as `do` of `by`
    is: ended       nothing more to do
    is: escalating  handed to a human, with the `reason`; under their `ruling` the
                    `stage` runs again

  `harness` is the harness the worker of the position is a session of, null where no
  worker acts: the stage's — the one it names, or the default of this checkout's
  config — for a worker that performs it, and the default for a consult and a landing.

  `valid` is false for what may not be declared where the issue is: a `reject` from a
  stage with no `on.reject`, a `ruling` nobody asked for, anything but `ruling` or
  `escalate` from a consult. It is no valid verdict, and the issue escalates with that
  as its reason. Anything declared on an issue that is landing or ended, and anything
  but a `ruling` on one that is escalating, is not valid either and changes nothing.

  `rework` is the issue's rework in total: its rejections and its consults' rulings.
  `consecutive_rework` is, for each stage that may reject, its rejections in a row: a
  `pass` of that stage starts its count over. A human's `ruling` starts every count
  over. `breaker` is null, or the breaker this step tripped — "max_rework" or
  "max_consecutive_rework": the rework that would have passed it is refused and not
  counted, and the issue escalates at the stage that would have run again.

  Errors, as {"error": …} with a non-zero exit: a word that is none of the six; the
  team has no workflow of that name; the team has a fault — {"error", "faults"}, as
  `check` answers it; and every error of `check`.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - It rehearses only a team that loads. Run `check` first.
  - A count never passes its breaker. With `max_rework: 3` the fourth rework is the one
    refused: the step says `"breaker": "max_rework"` and `"rework": 3`.
  - Running again after `blocked` is no rework.
