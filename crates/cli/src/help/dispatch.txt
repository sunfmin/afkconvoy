WHEN TO RUN IT

  To start a worker on one issue by hand. A tick does this on its own for every frontier
  issue it has a free slot for, and for every claim of its own left without a worker. Do
  it yourself when a plan shows an issue the fleet is not starting, or — with `--start
  fresh` — to throw away an attempt that went wrong and have the issue worked again.
  Run `recovery` first: it shows what this would continue from.

WHO RUNS IT

  The operator, in a checkout of the repo, on the host the worker is to run on.

WHAT IT READS AND WRITES

  Before it claims an issue the instance does not hold, it reads the issue's blockers on
  GitHub once more: with any of them open nothing is claimed and no worker starts. A
  tick's rebuild can be minutes older than its dispatches, and a blocker added in
  between holds the issue back all the same.

  It claims the issue for `--instance` unless that instance holds it already, looks at
  what survives of an earlier attempt, has orca put a worktree there, records the
  worktree's branch in a comment on the issue, writes the worker's brief into the
  worktree, starts the worker launch command in it, and hands the worker its prompt.

  In a bound repo the issue's flow label names its workflow, and the worker performs
  the stage the issue's stage records put it at — the workflow's first while there are
  none — in the stage's role, with the verdicts declared so far, and the reason of a
  rejection, in its brief. The team's repo and each role's are
  asked for the commit at their head; one the fleet has no copy of yet is cloned and
  kept, read-only, under the cache directory (copies/<owner>/<repo>/<commit>; `afkconvoy
  help cache` says where that directory is and what moves it), once however many roles it holds. A role that is a subdirectory of its repo
  is copied out of that on its own (<commit>-role-<path>; <commit>-role+codex-<path>,
  with a link to the role's skills where Codex finds them, for a Codex worker), and so
  is the team's
  directory, without the directories of its roles and without a `.claude/` at its top
  (<commit>-team+rules-<path>): a worker is given its role's directory and, of the
  team's, the team rules (its `CLAUDE.md`) — nothing else above its role, and no
  neighbour's.
  The terminal is opened on the worktree as ever, and the line it runs
  changes to the role's copy, puts the stage's facts in the environment (AFKCONVOY_ISSUE,
  _FLOW, _STAGE, _ROLE, _SKILL, _WRITES, _ROLE_COMMIT, _TEAM_COMMIT, _HARNESS), and
  starts the worker launch command with the worktree and the team's copy added as
  directories, the user's own settings left out, and the stage's model when it names
  one — as Claude Code takes them; what a pi worker and a Codex worker are started
  with is in `afkconvoy run --help`. The worker's harness is the one the stage names, or the default of the repo's
  config; a consult's and a landing worker's is the default.

  Where the stage declared `escalate` and names a consult, the worker is started as
  that consult instead: in the consult's role, on a brief that names the skill, the
  stage that asked and its reason, and the two lines it ends with — `afkconvoy rule`,
  or `afkconvoy verdict escalate` to let it through to a human. AFKCONVOY_CONSULT is
  `true` in its environment, the guard refuses its commits, and the stage's model is
  not its own.

  Once the issue's last stage has passed to `land`, the worker is started in the
  workflow's landing role instead, on the join-only brief with that role's landing skill
  named in it: it gates the issue's open PR and joins the landing train. Its stage is
  `land`, and it declares no verdict.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

  {"issue", "started": true, "claim", "tier", "action", "prompt", "reason", "worktree",
   "branch", "terminal"}

    claim      won (claimed now) or held (the instance's already)
    tier, action
               1 reuse_worktree — the worktree is still on this host; 2 recreate_at_tip
               — a new worktree at the issue's pushed branch; 3 dispatch_fresh — a new
               worktree at the base branch
    prompt     the brief the worker got: fresh, continue, or join (its PR is open: only
               the join is left) — in a bound repo, stage, consult, or join for a
               landing worker
    stage      in a bound repo: {flow, name, role, skill, writes, consult, harness, model,
               role_commit, role_dir, team_commit, team_dir} — the stage the worker
               performs, and the copies and commits fixed for it; name is `land` for a
               landing worker. For a consult (`consult: true`), name is the stage that
               asked, and role and skill are the consult's
    reason     why that tier, in words
    discarded  with `--start fresh`: {closed_prs, deleted_branches, removed_worktree}

  {"issue", "started": false, "claim": "lost", "owner"} — another instance holds the
  claim. Nothing was started.

  {"issue", "started": false, "skipped": "blocked", "blocked_by"} — the issue has an
  open blocker: blocked_by lists them, lowest number first. Nothing was claimed and
  nothing was started.

  Errors: the issue is closed; in a bound repo, the issue carries no flow label, more
  than one, or one that names no workflow of the team, it is at no stage a worker
  performs (the error says where it is), it passed to `land` and no open PR closes it, the team does not load (the error carries
  its `faults`) —
  none of which claims the issue; the instance's claim on it is one a
  stage already declared under; the worker launch
  command resolves to nothing; orca
  fails, or the worker is not ready in time. After a failed step the claim is still
  held: the instance's next tick finds a claim with no worker and continues it.

WHAT TRIPS YOU UP

  - `--instance` is required, and it is the instance that holds the claim afterwards.
    Give it the id of the fleet that is to look after the worker.
  - `--start fresh` discards the previous attempt: it closes the issue's open PRs,
    deletes its branches on the remote and removes its worktree, unpushed work included.
  - It checks that the issue is open and, unless the instance holds it already, that no
    blocker of it is open — nothing else of the frontier: an issue without the ready
    label, or past the concurrency, is started all the same. A claim the instance holds
    is continued whatever blocks it.
  - A role in a subdirectory is not the directory a human opens in a checkout: a
    `CLAUDE.md` or a `.claude/` above it there is not in the worker's copy, nor above it
    — but for the `CLAUDE.md` of the team's own directory, the team rules, which every
    worker is given. `afkconvoy check` notes each role that has another above it.
  - It does not ask after a worker already there. On a claim whose worker is at work it
    starts another in the same worktree.
