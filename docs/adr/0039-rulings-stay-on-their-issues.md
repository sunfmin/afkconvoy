# Rulings stay on their issues; lasting rules use ordinary work

A ruling is the decision on its issue, and nothing more. There is no separate precedent authority: it repeated rules already owned by ADRs, teams and roles, and promoting a ruling into it added a worker, worktree, claim, PR and human merge to every ruling. That lifecycle is removed, not moved into another dedicated workflow. An enduring rule is amended through ordinary implementation in its owning document — an ADR, a team's or a role's rules — with conflicting old wording amended and source links kept.

An actual human instruction or confirmation and its source establish human intent. A shared GitHub identity, an agent-authored ruling, or a merged document alone establishes none. Actual user decisions quoted in issues or ADRs remain binding. Ruling parsing, consult permissions, conflict resolution and the existing escalation boundaries remain, neither widened nor narrowed.

## Consequences

- A team's `precedents` key is refused, with what to do instead: move its effective rules and their sources into their owning documents, then remove the key. No proposer role or skill is loaded, and no precedent text is injected into a brief.
- What an older release left of proposals is only retired. A proposal's claim or run keeps no fleet from idle and holds back no ordinary issue, patrol or cut work; a tick retires it, leased to the sha it read, and leaves a live peer's alone. A legacy proposal PR gets one disposition comment pointing its content to ordinary work, and is left open for review; nothing polls, accepts or merges proposals.
